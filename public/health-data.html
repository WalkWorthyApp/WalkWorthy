Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 4, 2026

This policy supplements the WalkWorthy Privacy Policy and describes how Nathan Jones, the operator of WalkWorthy, handles consumer health data. WalkWorthy provides general spiritual encouragement and personal reflection; it is not a medical, mental-health, diagnostic, treatment, or crisis service.

Consumer health data we collect

When you choose to submit a check-in, we collect:

  • Your mood score and mood label.
  • Emotion tags and selected life-impact areas, which can include faith.
  • Your follow-up score and any optional note you write with the check-in.
  • The date and check-in period (morning, midday, or evening).

We derive mood summaries, Scripture-based encouragements, and daily reflections from that information, including inferred overall sentiment. These records are linked to your account identifier. Optional text can reveal health conditions, religious beliefs, or identifying details. Journal entries are stored on your device and are not collected by us in the current iOS app. Any cloud journal entries created through older clients remain subject to access and deletion; new cloud journal writes are disabled.

Sources

We receive consumer health data directly from the choices and optional text you submit in WalkWorthy. We create the derived summaries and generated responses described above from those submissions. We do not buy consumer health data from data brokers or obtain it from social networks, advertisers, public records, or health-care providers.

Why we collect and use it

  • To save and show the private check-in and mood history you request.
  • To generate the encouragement and daily reflection you request.
  • To screen optional notes and generated prose for safety.
  • To protect your health data, prevent unauthorized access and comply with applicable law.

Submitting a check-in requests storage of that check-in and its derived history in your private account. Before OpenAI safety screening or generation, we ask for an affirmative sharing choice. Optional profile personalization has a separate control. We use authenticated cloud storage, deterministic summaries and automated AI processing for the purposes described here. We will not collect or use additional categories of consumer health data, or use this data for a new purpose, without first updating this policy and obtaining any consent required by law.

Who receives it

  • Google Firebase / Google Cloud processes check-ins, derived mood summaries, generated responses, account identifiers, and related request/security data to host and secure your private account data.
  • OpenAI processes mood scores and labels, emotion tags, life areas, follow-up scores, check-in period and an optional note for safety screening and generation only after you turn on “Share check-ins with OpenAI.” Daily reflections use a seven-day summary of dates, morning/midday/evening mood levels and overall sentiment. If profile personalization is also on, it may receive an adult age range, occupation or major, and hobbies. We do not deliberately include your account name, email, Firebase user ID, gender, or on-device journal. Optional text you enter can nevertheless contain those details.

We also disclose health data when applicable law requires it, subject to the legal protections for that data. We do not use health data for advertising or permit health data collection through the app to track you across unrelated sites or services.

These providers process data for the purposes disclosed above. We have no affiliates with whom we share consumer health data. We do not sell consumer health data or disclose it to data brokers, advertisers, social networks, or other third parties for their independent use.

Your consumer health data rights

  • Access and confirmation — view your check-ins and generated responses in the app, or email us to request confirmation, a copy, or a list of recipients, their contact information, and the categories of consumer health data shared with them as required by applicable law.
  • Withdraw permission — turn off “Share check-ins with OpenAI” to stop new OpenAI requests once the withdrawal is saved on the server. If saving fails, AI stops on that device and you can retry or contact us; cross-device withdrawal requires server confirmation. Already transmitted processing cannot reliably be recalled. Turn off profile personalization separately to stop future use of those details. You may also email us to withdraw collection or sharing permission. You can stop submitting check-ins and retain access to existing history and local journaling.
  • Review or correct — update your profile in Settings or email us to review or correct other health data, including a disputed derived record.
  • Delete — Settings → Account → Delete Account removes your account, cloud check-ins, summaries, generated responses, permission records, legacy cloud journals and local data after successful completion. You may also email us with a deletion request.
  • Appeal — if we deny a request, reply to our decision with “Appeal” in the subject line and explain why you believe the decision should be reconsidered.

Submit a request to walkworthyofficial@gmail.com. We may ask for information reasonably needed to authenticate the request. General health-data rights requests receive a response without undue delay and within 45 days. Where legally permitted and reasonably necessary, we may extend once by another 45 days and explain the extension during the initial period. We apply a shorter period where required.

Nevada deletion: we delete consumer health data and notify applicable processors and other recipients within 30 days after authenticating a deletion request; notified recipients must delete within 30 days after notice. We also direct deletion by applicable processors, contractors and recipients for Washington requests. For archived or backup copies, any permitted delay is bounded by applicable law: no more than six months after authentication for Washington requests, or two years for Nevada requests. These are legal outer limits, not routine retention targets; a shorter applicable deadline controls.

Appeals: we explain our decision in writing within 45 days after receiving your appeal. If we deny it, we explain the reason and how to contact your Attorney General. You may contact the Washington Attorney General or Nevada Attorney General directly. Requests are free at least twice per year; we only charge a fee or decline an excessive request when applicable law permits it, and explain the basis. We do not discriminate against you for exercising these rights.

Retention and security

Cloud check-ins, summaries, profile data, and generated responses are kept until you delete your account or exercise an applicable deletion right. A minimal separate deletion-security record prevents data from being recreated during cleanup; completed records are marked to expire after seven days and contain no health content. Unfinished deletion records remain until resolved; failed cleanup is automatically retried. Device data removal starts when you confirm deletion and resumes after a restart if interrupted. A minimal local account identifier and cleanup status track unresolved cleanup. Automatic server retries require a saved deletion request; retry or contact support if the app cannot confirm acceptance. Provider copies and legally necessary security records are subject to applicable retention/deletion rules and the deadlines above. Disabling OpenAI response storage and tracing does not eliminate standard provider abuse-monitoring retention; see OpenAI API data controls. We use access controls, authenticated requests, App Check, HTTPS, owner-scoped database rules, rate limits, data minimization, and protected local storage as described in the main Privacy Policy. No system can guarantee perfect security.

Changes to this policy

We publish the current policy here with its effective date. Material changes will be communicated in-app or by email before taking effect. New health-data categories or purposes require the notice and affirmative consent applicable law requires; continued use alone does not replace that consent.
